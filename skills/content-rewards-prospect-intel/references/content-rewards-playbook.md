## 1. What CR is

Content is abundant. Distribution and attention are scarce, and coordinated, measurable distribution is scarcer
still. CR is the infrastructure that lets a brand pay many creators and clippers per verified view, per post or
on retainer. Each campaign comes with a written brief, approvals, bot scoring, reporting and payouts. An agency can
run the campaign if the brand doesn't want to operate it.

Verified mechanics (cite these pages):
- **Pricing:** $1,000 minimum, a 10% platform fee (8% if verified), no subscription, top up any time
  (`/pricing`).
- **Payout models:** CPM, per post, retainer and pool. Each platform has its own rate and a min and max payout per
  clip, so budget spreads across many clips instead of one viral outlier (campaign pages).
- **Approval:** nothing approves automatically. There's a 7-day review window, and rejections must cite the
  written brief. Pre-post draft review is available (`/agencies` FAQ, `/brands-terms` §5.6, §7).
- **Fraud:** every submission gets a bot-likelihood score before approval, and flagged payouts are held for review.
  Independent third-party view verification is in beta with selected agencies (`/agencies` FAQ).
- **Geography:** campaigns can require audience location. Live examples are titled "[35%+GERMANY]" and
  "[35%+FRANCE]" (Clipping Culture), and briefs ask creators to "share audience demographics".
- **Reporting:** views, approved submissions, likes, comments and effective CPM by campaign, platform and day,
  with CSV export and a live client link (`/agencies` FAQ).
- **Licensing:** approved clips are licensed to the brand perpetually and royalty-free, including use in paid
  ads (`/brands-terms` §12.2). Winning clips can become Spark or partnership ads.
- **Agencies:** one agency account can hold many client brands, with roles. More than 200 agencies are on the
  platform (company claim).
- **Observed CPMs:** most live campaigns sit at $0.50 to $2 per 1K. Music and logo campaigns run as low as $0.05.
  Premium campaigns (finance, geo-targeted) run $2.70 to $3+. In one worked example, Michael Sartain's campaign
  spent $6,841 for 4.5M views, an effective CPM of about $1.52.

## 2. The perception chain

The AE sells what the views are meant to communicate, not the views themselves.

```
Business outcome → behavior needed → belief/perception needed → narrative → content →
creator archetype → account architecture → platform → mechanic → spend → measurement → iteration
```

Example: "we want 30M views" is the wrong starting point. "Increase trial among 18-25 men" needs people to
recognize the product in a store. That needs the belief "this is what everyone at the gym drinks now". That's
delivered through gym, gaming and late-night-work narratives, fitness creators (utility), lifestyle creators
(desire), small relatable creators (normality), meme and clip pages (ubiquity) and a few big names (legitimacy).
Only then do CPM and budget come in.

Marty Supreme is a useful reference. The film's jacket travelled through athletes, musicians and celebrities
until it became the campaign itself (Istituto Marangoni: "How the Nahmias x Marty Supreme jacket became the
film's entire marketing campaign"). Memes and the Chalamet/EsDeeKid rumor added more nodes. Many nodes reinforced
one cultural object, and the film felt like an event. Don't quote engagement figures unless you've opened the
original analysis.
CR doesn't guarantee that outcome. It's the infrastructure that makes that style of distributed storytelling
operable at scale.

## 3. Objectives taxonomy

ubiquity · mental availability · launch eventization · product education · founder authority · credibility/social
proof · status signaling · cultural relevance · narrative change · app installs · follower growth · qualified
traffic · UGC supply (assets for ads) · community growth · demand capture · sales.

Map each one to a measurement: search lift, branded traffic, follower velocity, installs, code redemptions,
sentiment, share of voice, effective CPM, and the content winners that get licensed into paid ads.

## 4. Wedges (pick one primary)

| Wedge | Evidence pattern |
|---|---|
| Content but no distribution | Lots of podcast, YouTube or livestream hours (supply HIGH or EXTREME); the short-form feed is thin or only on the main account |
| Paid-heavy, organic-thin | Many active Meta or TikTok ads, `facebook-domain-verification` in DNS, a weak owned feed, no constellation |
| Founder goldmine | A charismatic founder with interviews and talks; the founder's clips outperform the product posts |
| Launch or eventization | A dated catalyst within 90 days (drop, release, season, tour, film) |
| UGC supply | DTC or ecommerce, Bazaarvoice or reviews in the stack, many products, needs demos and testimonials at volume |
| App installs | An app in the stores, lifestyle or consumer use case, comparable CR app campaigns (Cantina, Scroll The Bible, Lovable) |
| Already clipping, scale it | A constellation exists, or they already appear as a CR or Whop campaign; the pitch is infrastructure, measurement, more budget and the right agency |
| Agency or ops pain | They run creators manually (an influencer manager role, spreadsheets, a GRIN or Aspire stack) and the pain is payouts, tracking and fraud |
| Low fit | B2B with no audience, regulated claims they can't let creators make, or no content angle. Say so. |

## 6. Brief design: coordinated variation

A great brief doesn't tell 500 creators to make the same ad. It gives them enough strategic alignment to produce
different expressions of the same objective. Requirements must be written in the campaign fields. CR rejections
must cite the brief, and rules shared on Discord aren't enforceable (`/brands-terms` §5.6).

Build the starter brief from what already works for the prospect:
- Founder clips outperform → use founder footage as the source; require hooks in the first 2 seconds.
- Product demos outperform → creator demos, problem-and-solution, before-and-after.
- Memes and culture outperform → meme-native formats, trend audio allowed (licensing permitting).
- Long-form exists → clip educational, emotional, contrarian or high-information moments.

Always include: format (9:16, length range), captions burned in, disclosure tags, the claims creators must not
make, the account rules (dedicated page, tag @brand in the bio), the minimum view threshold, geo/demographic share,
and a link to reference material.

## 7. Choosing the mechanic, operator and agency

- **CPM:** you want volume and distribution density, and you have plenty of source material.
- **Per post:** you need guaranteed asset volume (UGC for ads), or a niche where views are spiky.
- **Retainer:** you need always-on dedicated creators (the FOMO x Clip Farm private retainer was $500/mo).
- **Self-serve vs agency:** use an agency when the brand lacks short-form ops, needs a curated creator pool,
  multi-language or geo targeting, or fast scale.
- **Agency fit:** match on public campaign history from `/c/HANDLE`. Clip Farm covers apps, fintech, podcasts,
  sports and consumer brands. Clipping Culture covers music labels, gaming and geo-targeted work. The Clip Ship
  covers music, film and TV. Artist Influence covers music releases. ClipHaus covers gaming, crypto and brand
  collaborations. Virality and Propaganda run high-volume, broad work. Phrase it as "based on public campaign
  history, X looks most relevant because...". Never imply anything about availability, contracts or internal
  performance.

## 8. Objections with evidence-based answers

| Objection | Direction of answer | Don't claim | Follow-up question |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Are these views bots?" | Every submission is bot-scored before approval, flagged payouts are held, and nothing auto-approves. Independent view verification is in beta. Brands pay per verified view and approve each clip. | "Zero bots" or "guaranteed real" | "What would you need to see in the first week to trust the numbers?" |
| "Wrong countries or demographics" | Campaigns can require audience geography (live "35%+ Germany" campaigns) and ask for demographic screenshots. Per-platform rates can differ. | That the targeting is precise like paid ads | "Which markets matter, and what share would you call success?" |
| "Low-quality or off-brand content" | The written brief governs approvals, there's pre-post draft review, the brand rejects anything off-brief within 7 days, and min/max payout per clip applies. | That every clip will be on-brand | "What's a hard no for your brand?" |
| "Views don't equal sales" | Agree. Views are the delivery unit. Set the outcome first, measure search lift, branded traffic, installs and codes, then scale what moves. Winning clips are licensed for paid ads. | Any revenue forecast | "If this worked, what number would your CFO look at?" |
| "Why not just Meta ads?" | Different job. Organic distribution through many creator contexts builds mental availability and supplies proven creative to paid. Compare CPMs, labelled as context. | That it replaces paid | "Where does creative for paid come from today?" |
| "We have an influencer program" | Influencer posts are a few big bets. CR is many paid-per-result posts with tracking and payouts in one place. It can complement or absorb the program. | That influencers don't work | "How do you pay and track creators today?" |
| "Who runs it?" | Self-serve, or matched with an agency with relevant public history. | Agency capacity or outcomes | "Do you have short-form ops in-house?" |
| "Can we use our existing footage?" | Yes. Clipping campaigns are built on brand-provided long-form, and the brand owns clips made from its footage (§12.5). | Music or talent rights are cleared | "What footage do you own the rights to?" |
| "Disclosure / FTC" | Briefs require disclosure tags, and CR has an FTC compliance page. | Legal advice | "Does legal need to review the brief template?" |
| "Refunds or unspent budget" | Unspent budget can be refunded after review (5-7 business days), after approved payouts are deducted. Note the right to hold up to 20% (§10.4) and say so honestly if asked. | "Instant refunds" | |

## 9. Discovery question patterns

Write questions that cite what you found:

- "Your founder's interview clips get roughly N times the views of product posts, but they only go out on the main
  account. Have you tested distributing that library through dedicated clip pages?"
- "You're running about N active Meta ads, but your TikTok posts twice a week. Is the bottleneck creative,
  distribution, or that paid is easier to measure?"
- "You launch X on <date>. Is the goal direct response, or making the launch feel unavoidable that week?"
- "We found N accounts already posting your clips. Are those yours, an agency's, or organic fans?"
- "What should your audience be saying about you three months from now that they don't say today?"
- "When you've increased creator spend before, what caused it?"

Also cover budget, decision process, timeline, existing agency relationships, measurement stack, and rights to
footage.

## 10. Money-math framing and expansion logic

- Separate distribution math (budget → creator budget after fee → views at the CPM) from business math (labelled
  SCENARIO, with stated assumptions).
- Right-size budget to strategy instead of the smallest comfortable test: "If the goal is to own the launch window
  with 18-24 year olds, what creator diversity and distribution density would make that plausible?"
- Expansion path: fund, observe, keep the winning narratives and creators, top up, add platforms or geos, then
  license winners to paid. Once you know what works, scaling is an allocation decision rather than a leap of faith.

## 11. Language

Use: market perception, narrative distribution, distributed storytelling, creator-native, mental availability,
coordinated organic distribution, cultural surface area, distribution density.

Avoid: "manipulate consumers", "make it look organic" (if it means undisclosed), "guaranteed viral",
"bot-free", made-up case results, and naming CR customers that aren't public.

## 12. The supply side: how clippers actually work

Knowing the clipper toolchain lets the AE set honest expectations and spot farmed output. Every tool below is
public open source, verified on 2026-10-08.

**The six-step chain:**

| Step | What happens | Common OSS tooling |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Source | pull the long-form episode, VOD or podcast | yt-dlp |
| 2. Transcribe | word-timed transcript, speaker labels | WhisperX (23.5k stars), FunClip (Alibaba), whisper.cpp |
| 3. Pick moments | LLM scores 30-90s windows for hook, standalone sense and payoff | AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator, openshorts (5.3k), autoclip (8.9k), claude-shorts |
| 4. Reframe | 16:9 to 9:16 with face tracking or split screen | OpenCV/mediapipe pipelines inside the tools above |
| 5. Caption and dub | burned-in word captions, translation, dubbing | KrillinAI, VideoLingo, pyvideotrans |
| 6. Post | schedule across accounts and platforms | Postiz (official OAuth, legitimate); cookie-based multi-account uploaders (red flag) |

**Expectations to set with the brand:**
- Volume: a typical episode-hour has a handful of genuinely strong moments, not 100. Big clip counts come from
  re-cuts (different hooks, captions or ratios of the same moment) and from the same clip posted on many accounts.
  That's fine for distribution density, as long as the brand knows. Ask about approved clips per episode.
- Quality: the most common visible failures are a clip that needs earlier context, and a two-person podcast where
  speaker tracking shows the wrong face. The fix is a brief that asks for standalone hooks and a draft review.
- Geo campaigns: clippers meet "35%+ Germany"-style requirements partly by dubbing or subtitling clips into the
  local language (VideoLingo, pyvideotrans). Ask how the brand wants localized versions handled.
- Faceless supply: MoneyPrinterTurbo (124k stars) and Pixelle-Video generate topic-to-video output (AI script,
  stock footage, TTS, subtitles). That's a different product from clipping a real creator. Use a "real footage
  only" line in the brief if the brand cares.

**Red flags of farmed or botted supply** (what to look for when a brand asks "are these views real?"):
- Many accounts with near-identical bios, handles that differ only by a number or suffix, the same link-in-bio,
  and creation dates bunched together.
- Machine-regular posting times. The same caption template and hook text across accounts.
- High views with almost no comments, saves or profile visits. Sudden spikes with no follower growth.
- Audience geography that doesn't match the brief.

CR's counter-measures: a bot-likelihood score on every submission, held payouts while a flag is reviewed, brief-
based rejection, demographic-share requirements, and independent view verification in beta.

**Clip scoring rubric the AE can borrow** (from claude-shorts): hook 30%, standalone coherence 25%, emotional
intensity 20%, value density 15%, payoff 10%. `scripts/moments.py` approximates it from a transcript.
